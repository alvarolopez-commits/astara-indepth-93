In Depth #92
Charging · Fuel · Fleets · 40 Markets

More plugs
than pumps

Public charging points, fuel stations and the fleets they serve in 40 markets

Prepared 23 October 2026 ≈12 plugs per station
00Summary

The plug network has outgrown the pump network

The plug network has already outgrown the pump network, but the two do not do the same job. In 23 of the 25 markets where both can be counted, there are more public charging points than fuel stations: 6,824,410 points against 546,878 stations, about 12 to 1. Only India and Brazil still have more pumps than plugs.

Per vehicle the gap is even wider. In the median market, 13 electric cars share each public charging point, while 1,892 combustion vehicles share each fuel station. That does not mean electric drivers are over-served. A fuel station has several pumps and refills a car in minutes; a slow public point serves one car for hours. And most electric cars charge at home: the world has more than 43 million private points, six for every public one.

The network is also extremely concentrated. China holds 4,680,000 public points, 67% of the world total, and roughly four in five of the fast ones. Outside China, the markets that build the most public points per EV are not the ones with the most EVs: Norway, where 39.1% of cars are electric, has 34.0 EVs per public point; the Netherlands has 5.8.

01The Network

Seven million public plugs, and six times as many at home

The International Energy Agency counts more than 7 million public charging points worldwide at the end of 2025, after nearly 1.8 million were added in a single year, a rise of more than a third. The IEA country table behind that headline adds up to 6.97 million, because country values are rounded; the two figures are kept apart and never averaged.

Public points are the visible part of the network. The IEA estimates more than 43 million private points in homes and workplaces, for about 76 million electric light-duty vehicles. On the global average, 11 electric vehicles share each public point, with 4.5 kW of public charging capacity per vehicle.

The latest national series move faster than the IEA table. China's National Energy Administration counted 5.16 million public points at the end of August 2026; the EU-27 reached 1,159,838 in August 2026 according to the European Alternative Fuels Observatory.

02Charging Points

Where the plugs are

China alone has 4,680,000 public points, 46.6% of them fast (above 22 kW). South Korea follows with 491,170, and only 10.4% of them are fast. The United States has 240,000, 29.2% fast. The Netherlands, with 207,300, has more public points than Germany or France but only 3.5% fast ones.

03Fuel Stations

Where the pumps are

No one publishes a world census of fuel stations. In the 29 of 40 markets where a defensible count exists, there are 583,047 stations. The pump network is old, dense and shrinking slowly:

  • Europe: FuelsEurope counts 138,831 stations across the EU, UK, Norway, Switzerland and Türkiye in 2025 (Finland, Norway and Germany report older years). Italy has the most (21,700), ahead of Germany (14,376) and Spain (12,746).
  • United States: 122,620 convenience stores sold fuel at the end of 2025, the highest number in eight years. They sell about 80% of consumer fuel, so the full count of outlets is higher.
  • China: about 110,000 stations at the end of 2025, 7.5% fewer than at the 2021 peak.
  • India: 100,266 retail outlets at the end of November 2025, double the 50,451 of 2015 and the only large market where the pump network is still growing fast.
  • Japan: 27,009 stations at the end of March 2025, down from 29,005 four years earlier. Canada: 11,465 at the end of 2025, the lowest since the late 1980s.
  • Brazil: 45,879 stations at the end of 2025.
04Fleets

The fleets behind them

Even in the leading markets, combustion cars are still most of the fleet. The electric share of the car stock (BEV plus PHEV, plug-in hybrids included) is:

Norway leads with 39.1%, followed by Denmark and Iceland at about 22%. China reaches 12.0% of all automobiles. The United States is at 2.3%, Japan at 1.0% and Brazil at 0.5%.

Korea and Türkiye do not split plug-in hybrids from conventional hybrids, so their EV figure is battery-electric only and PHEVs remain in the combustion column.

05Ratios

Two ratios, side by side

For the 23 markets with all four figures, two simple ratios can be set next to each other: electric cars per public charging point, and combustion vehicles per fuel station.

The median market has 13 EVs per public point and 1,892 combustion vehicles per station. Korea has the densest public network (1.7 EVs per point) and Norway the thinnest of the European leaders (34.0). On the combustion side, Norway and Denmark have the fewest vehicles per station, France and the United Kingdom the most.

Counted site against point, the plug network has overtaken the pump network in 23 of 25 markets. The Netherlands has 50.2 public points per fuel station and China 42.5. India (0.9) and Brazil (0.4) are the only markets where stations still outnumber public points.

06Caveats

Why a plug is not a pump

The two ratios measure different things:

Unit. A charging point charges one vehicle at a time. A fuel station is a site with several pumps, each with several nozzles. No country publishes harmonised pump or nozzle counts, so no "equivalent" ratio can be built without estimating, and none is.

Time. A fast charger needs tens of minutes; a slow one, hours. A fuel stop takes minutes. Fewer vehicles per point is therefore expected, not a sign of over-supply.

Home charging. With more than 43 million private points, most charging never touches the public network. Where most drivers can charge at home, fewer public points per EV are needed.

Fewer vehicles per point is therefore expected, not a sign of over-supply.

From the analysis

That is why the EV share of the fleet and the density of public charging do not move together. Norway, with the highest EV share, has 34.0 EVs per public point; Korea, with a fleet share of 4.1%, has 1.7. The data show the gap; they do not explain it.

07What to Watch

What to watch

  1. 01
    China · monthly charging data

    China's NEA publishes charging-facility counts monthly; the jump from 4.68 m (IEA, end-2025) to 5.16 m (NEA, August 2026) shows how fast the national series moves.

  2. 02
    European Union · AFIR

    The EU's AFIR regulation sets charging-power targets per registered EV; Transport & Environment says the EU as a whole exceeds the fleet-based target by more than 180%.

  3. 03
    Fuel retail · closures

    Station closures: Japan, Canada and China all report slow, steady declines in the pump network; India is the exception.

  4. 04
    IEA · next outlook

    The IEA publishes its Global EV Outlook every spring; the next edition will update public points and EV stock for end-2026.
